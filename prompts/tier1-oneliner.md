# Tier 1: one-liner

A controlled experiment on the setup. Change one thing per run. It contains no idea, so
results rhyme with everyone else's: use it to prove the pipeline, then move up a tier.

## Base

```
Make a dynamic 15-second motion graphics showreel, the kind a motion designer puts on a résumé,
showing off what you can do. A new technique every shot. Go all out.
```

Why each part matters: "15-second" gives room for 6–8 shots and still finishes in one pass.
"Showreel" is a genre with known rules (best work first, constant variety). Making the model the
subject means there's no product to get wrong. "Go all out" asks for effort on top of the setting.

## Variants (one change each)

```
# Length + sound bar
A 60-second 16:9 showreel that pushes your creative range. Sound design matters as much as
picture: compose an original piano score in code and cut every shot to it. No generic pads.

# Anti-slop guardrail
A 10-second motion piece introducing yourself as Opus 5.5. No text or labels in the corners,
no frame borders: the usual tells of AI-made video.

# Story instead of techniques
Showreel energy, but tell a story: the history of [TOPIC] from [START] to today. You choose
the storyboard. 45 seconds, vertical 9:16.

# Agency persona
A 30-second reel for an imaginary branding studio for startup founders. Every graphic made
from scratch, one accent colour, every shot a different technique.
```
